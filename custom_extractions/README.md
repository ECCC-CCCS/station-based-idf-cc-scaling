# CCCS Data Products Office: Custom Extractions Repository

This repository contains a tracker of custom extraction requests of DPO, including go/no-go considerations (see below), active project tracking, finished project archives, and optionally actually extraction code.

# Custom Analysis Go/No-Go Considerations

## Objective
The Data Products Office (DPO) is periodically asked to perform so-called ‘custom extractions’ of climate data, which involve work to develop climate data indices or regional dataset that are beyond those available or producible on ClimateData.ca, or the Analysis page.   These requests come from various sources, including direct requests from OGDs, provincial/territorial governments, or CCCS partner agencies, and Support Desk inquiries.

The decision to undertake particular custom extractions is currently made on a case-by-case basis.  However, increasing volume and complexity of custom extraction requests, and considerations related to the joint public/private sector nature of many such requests, suggests the need for general, documented DPO ‘go/no-go’ considerations that equitably and objectively support the decision on whether (or not) to undertake the requested extraction.  These considerations will help standardize the decision process around particular custom extraction requests.  They will also provide external transparency around DPO’s decision making process, particularly where DPO declines to perform a custom extraction for a requesting party.

## Overview

The following DPO custom extraction go/no-go considerations – framed in the form of yes/no questions - are not intended to be dogmatic or inflexible.  Rather, they provide a structured set of questions that should each be consciously considered in the context of custom extraction requests.  In principle, one may apply a documented threshold (e.g. >50%) to these considerations, to determine a ‘go’ decision for a particular custom extraction request.  Alternatively, in the interests of transparency, individual important considerations that are not satisfied may be communicated to the requesting party as justification for not undertaking the custom extraction.  Because of the varying nature of their use, the go/no-go considerations are simply listed here, and not (for example) weighted by importance or criticality.  Weighting should be done on a case-by-case basis.  **An answer of 'yes' to any of the following questions indicates support for the custom extraction.  An answer of 'no' suggests that the request should be more carefully considered before starting work.**

### Go/no-go Considerations
**Is the requested custom extraction appropriate to undertake?**
- Is it possible in principle to perform?
- Is it outside the defined/funded scope of work of another party (e.g. a consultant)? 
- Is the rationale for use of the data well articulated and well-posed from climate science and user needs perspectives? 
- Is the requested data difficult or impossible to obtain using available or soon-to-be-available ClimateData.ca or Analysis Page tools?

**Is the requested custom extraction scope of work reasonable?**
- Is it based on a reasonable schedule and time frame? 
- Will it take 2 weeks (75 hours) or less of effort?
- Can it be completed without unduly delaying other, higher-priority climate services delivery work? 
- Can pre-developed tools and workflows be applied to this custom extraction?

**Will undertaking the custom extraction benefit DPO, CCCS, or ECCC?**
- Will it result in generation of tools, capacity, or data that may be useful for future projects?
- Will it result in data and scripts that can be provided in an open data format to the public, outside the scope of the requesting party’s specific request?
- Will it provide information or data of benefit to other stakeholders?
- Will it result in a better CCCS understanding of user needs and climate data applications?
